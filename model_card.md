# Model card

## What the system is

Veil Pierce AI scores companies for indicators that their true ownership is being concealed, and writes a cited dossier that explains each score. It combines entity resolution, a knowledge graph, six deterministic detectors, an optional supervised model and a retrieval augmented narrative layer.

## Intended use

* Triage and prioritisation of corporate customers and counterparties for enhanced due diligence
* Support for analysts who need to see an ownership structure and the evidence behind it quickly
* Research and teaching on beneficial ownership transparency and on grounded language model systems

## Uses that are out of scope

* Automatic refusal, exit, freezing or reporting of any customer without review by a trained person
* Any decision about an individual based on a name only match
* Use as evidence that a person or company has committed an offence
* Use on data that the operator has no lawful basis to process

## Evaluation data

All reported results come from a synthetic registry with planted ground truth, produced by `veilpierce/data/synth.py` with a fixed seed. No real person or company is in the benchmark. Names are random combinations of common name tokens and words, and nationality and residence are sampled independently of the name pools so that no naming tradition is associated with risk.

## Results

The current figures are in the README and in `reports/metrics.json`. They are regenerated by `veilpierce benchmark`.

## Limitations

* The benchmark measures recovery of planted structure. It is not an estimate of performance on a live register.
* The public company data product lists live companies only. Features that depend on dissolved companies need archived snapshots or another source.
* Officer appointments are not available as a free bulk file.
* Leak data is dated, partial and unverified.
* Jurisdiction opacity weights are illustrative defaults and carry a judgement that each institution must make for itself.
* The narrative providers for hosted language models were exercised with scripted replies in the test suite. They were not run against a live model in the environment where this project was built.
* Name matching is tuned for names written in the Latin alphabet.

## Fairness and harm

The main risk of harm is a false alert on an innocent person, above all through a sanctions namesake match or a false merge in person resolution. The system limits that risk in four ways: date of birth is required before a match can be called confirmed, name only matches are capped at review level, every dossier lists all the spellings that were merged into one person so a reviewer can disagree, and the wording of every narrative is restricted to indicators and potential matches.

Country of residence and jurisdiction of registration are used as features because they are recognised risk factors in guidance on beneficial ownership. Nationality is never used.

## Human oversight

Every output is a recommendation to a reviewer. The dossier states this at the top, names the reviewer steps for each typology, and records which narrator wrote it and what the verifier removed.
